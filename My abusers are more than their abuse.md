### My abusers are more than their abuse 

_Your trigger warning, up front: this story contains sexual abuse. Please 
consume responsibly._

In trying to recover from my addiction, I was abused and neglected in every 
attempt at going to The Anonymous Clubs. The most egregious was my first 
sponsor. He asked me to stay until the end of the meeting, until everyone had 
left. I was broken, I was ashamed, I was vulnerable. And so: I was the prey. 
“I can tell from the look in your eyes,” my future sponsor said. “If I wanted you 
to, you would get down on your knees and suck my dick right now.” Part of me 
guffawed at the idea, half down for it because it sounded like a fun story. He 
told me later that he was just trying to get it out in the air. It must have been a 
toxic danger wherever it had been before. 

My sponsor had not actually asked me to suck his dick. Phew. All he really 
meant to do was groom me into someone he could control. I showed up to one 
meeting in a woman’s hoodie, and I did not pass inspection. He asked me to a 
funeral of one of the young men he had been the sponsor of in the past, and I 
said yes because I thought he needed a friend. He told me to wear black and 
handed me a hoodie with his running organization’s name on the front. I was 
not the only groupie, there were about six of us. He took a creepy picture of us 
all next to the dead young man’s photo. 

My sponsor gave me a lot of attention at first. Once he realized I was wild, 
ungroomable, he stopped responding to my calls when I would have the urge 
to use. I fell back into the warm embrace of my addiction, where I could be 
treated badly and everyone could understand why. 
He’s more than what he did to me. He’s a human  being who never figured out 
how to heal the root issues of his disease, but that did not take away from his 
accomplishments: the people he did help, the people he inspired, the people 
who got better with him. I know of one of them personally. I’m very proud of 
my friend who started training, running, reconnecting to their body. Stepping 
away from easy answers. 

I’d like to think I share that capacity to not take the easy answer.
