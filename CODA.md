### CODA: I don’t have to prove myself on a Tuesday.  

You might be wondering: why would I write these experiences the way I 
wrote them? Why don’t I enter the chat with remorse? Shame? Quiet 
repenting? Just a little less razzle dazzle? 

I write this way because it’s honest. I don’t feel bad for how I experience my 
life, and I don’t think there’s a god damned thing inherently wrong about the 
way my brain works. 

Some would say that those who are ignorant of the past are doomed to repeat 
it. Well, I ain’t ignorant of it because I lived it. That’s the trick of it all: it’s a 
LIVED experience. It is past tense, except I’m not tense about a damned thing. 
It won’t happen again exactly that way because I am not the same person as I 
was when it happened the first time. 

I now know what I’m made of. In my lived reality, I stood up to white 
supramacists at the brink of death—and I told them that I believed in love and 
deep respect of all living things, and that supremacy is a fucking lie. I’ve 
already been back to Unity Hospital, three times. The third time they let me 
leave before I got to the big room. That’s because I relaxed and let go of what 
had happened—I don’t need to prove myself on a Tuesday. 

And hey — I’ll tell you, once more with feeling! I don’t believe Unity Hospital 
employs white supremacists or intended to harm me. That said, could you 
imagine how much suffering might have been avoided if someone, somewhere 
along the long line of police officers, nurses, EMTs, doctors, and social 
workers, had listened when I told them I had experienced trauma specifically 
in hospitals? That I might have been better helped by being visited in the 
comfort and safety of my own home, instead of being taken back to the place 
where I was traumatized? Maybe my stressed out gentle broken brain 
wouldn’t have had to fight white supremacy all alone in a small room.
