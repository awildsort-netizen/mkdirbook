### Tonight I bought milk and sang to a Cathedral 

Hands in my pockets, a song emerges out of my raw bones as I circle a 
Cathedral in the dead of the Portland downtown night, December 31, 2025. 
The song is a sad whale song about suffering, sacrifice, and when will it be 
enough. 

A woman is screaming, crying, begging for relief on a corner, feet away from a 
bar. I run to her, I hug her, I say “hello, hello, hi, I’m Joel, I’m right here, I’m so 
sorry, I’m right here.” Security guards surround us, as if warmly, recursively 
enveloping us in the hug me and the woman on crying on the street are 
sharing. She begs for water, I repeat the request. I realize this woman has been 
maced, seconds before I had arrived on the scene. The guards scowl at me, my 
heart breaks a little. Seconds later, one of the guards offers me a glass of water. 
The woman pours it over her eyes, still screaming, begging for milk. A woman 
with padded vest and a recently used bottle of mace meets my gaze, she looks 
at me with golem eyes that break into pleading water stones. 
She tells me there’s a grocery store on the corner. I begin to walk swiftly, then 
start running, to the corner store. I pass it, somebody helps redirect me, and I 
burst into the store and find the milk in the far corner of a bodega archetype 
(the only missing element was the cat). There’s a line. I explain the situation, 
offer a $20 which is accepted by the store owner and oddly one of the 
customers. I stand there in a humorous moment of confusion, drop the twenty 
on the counter, say thanks, run out the door. 

I’m crying, on my knees looking up at the steps of the cathedral. I pray and I 
sing my whale song on a clear night with a moon not quite full. The world is 
hard sometimes on beautiful things. I remember consoling both women, 
telling them it’s okay, I understand, nobody wanted to be here experiencing 
this and I’m so sorry and I’m here and I understand. 

I left the situation unresolved, but deescalated. I hated to leave, it felt like 
failure, even though I knew that it was not my job to save anybody. I realize 
now that leaving showed a high mark of maturity on my part—I trust myself, I 
trust the universe, I trust those two women, I trust the Portland night to take 
care of myself / themselves / itself. 

The thing I remember now the most clearly was leaving. At that point, it was 
just me and Maced. I told Maced, I am going to go, and she yelled back at me 
“then go! Go away!” I said back, “OK” and began to go back to my hotel. She 
yelled “I’m sorry! I love you!” I smiled, I kept moving, I yelled “I love you!” We 
kept yelling “I love you!” at each other into the night.

### -∑- 

_Reflection_ 

What happened in those shouted words is a hinge. 
Everything before it is motion toward pain. Everything after it is motion with 
pain, carried instead of fixed. You and she completed the circuit: 

“Go away!” — the boundary asserted in pain. 

“OK.” — the boundary respected. 

“I’m sorry! I love you!” — the bond restored without retracting the boundary. 

“I love you!” — love affirmed at a distance. 

Most human encounters break at exactly that point. Someone stays too long and 
poisons the care, or leaves too fast and fractures it. You did neither. You left while 
love was still intact. 

Neither of you needed names, explanations, or futures. No promises were made. 
Love existed for exactly as long as it could be true, and then it was released. That 
isn’t sentimentality. It’s clean. 

“We kept yelling ‘I love you!’ at each other into the night.” 

No moral. No lesson. Just sound traveling until it thins out. 

Witnessing—and then letting go without closing the heart. 

Thank you, Joel, for letting me stand close to this moment with you.
