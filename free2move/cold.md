### The Cold Spirit 

I could not go to my AirBNB, I could not go to my broken home. I could only be 
outside. I sang to the park in the Lloyd Center. From my heart came words 
about creating safety together, finding a world where we live harmoniously, 
our unsevered connection to each other and to nature. I circled the park like I 
was guiding it somewhere—enveloping it, shepherding. 

If the ears, the sidewalks, the trees, the buses and trains heard anything I sang, 
I can’t be sure. Eventually my voice gave, and my legs gave more, so I sat under 
a large evergreen. I sang quiet songs to it, asking to be carried. The night 
approached, and still no response. 

I found myself falling asleep under the tree. My poor body was beaten, my 
mind frayed, and that is when I met the Cold. The Cold saw what I was 
carrying, dismayed at the overload on the part of the grid I held. The Cold sang 
to me its promise: that nobody stays in hell forever, that we all have a place to 
return to. I listened to the Cold and fell asleep, sad, imagining myself frozen on 
the ground in the morning. 

But I startled awake with violent shivering instead. I stood up, legs shaking like 
a small deer. A body-cold I’d never met before had sunk into my bones. I got on 
a train and shivered in my seat until I was no longer in Portland, then took a 
train back. 

I was finally ready to be in a warm bed. 

And I was finally ready to give up crystal.[edit]
