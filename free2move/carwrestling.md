**Car Wrestling**

I miss my Dad. I missed my dad so much the other day that I destroyed everything in my house, breaking the windows, being disrespectful of anything and everything that I’ve ever had the pleasure of owning, and confidently walked out of my house in boxer, briefs, and a T-shirt. 

“Not sad. Confident! Not crazy. Confident! Not wrong. Confident! I’m confident! I’m confident!” 

Grief does that sometimes—it puts on a suit that looks like certainty and dares you to argue with it. 

I learned something about the human spirit during a 4 mile walk heading west on Broadway Street through the middle of the night, on the Burnside Bridge, ending at the Walgreens on Burnside Avenue. 

1. Humans, even amidst a wide span of emotions, including anger, frustration, and simple dumbfounded awe, were incredibly determined to make sure that they did not hurt me. 
2. Humans really don’t want to get into a traffic accident. 

I did not walk down *the sidewalk* in my briefs and shirt—I walked down the **middle of the road**. Through traffic lights with buses and cars and a cops and ambulances. Most of them sped by. And then; I learned how to dance with them.

 I became a professional car wrestler, inventing an entire new career trajectory for myself. Here’s how easy it was: I would look at the traffic dead on, and I would walk right into it. The traffic would slow down. If it was able to escape my ironclad gaze, it would even signal left or right politely to let me know which way it was getting the fuck out of my way. 

I slowed cars down. I stopped them. I gave them a little ”honk honk hammy” on the nose (quite embarrassing for a car) and then I would whirlwind my arms and signal like I was a flight controller and send the car, humbled, on its dutiful way. 

Car wrestling! This is not an endorsement! Please read the next section!

**Wild Sort Operator** 

It’s not to say that we should be reckless. It’s not to put anyone in danger, but once something happens, then we can safely notice: look how stable and fluid our system response to something like a schizophrenic man who misses his dad can act in a highly unusual fashion, and stumble upon a deep undercurrent of respect, love, and tradition. 

I like to say it like: we live in a love field, and that field is powerful and does not require a force. Love is the path of least resistance. 

I’m writing this around the time of Christmas, and may I just say: happy holidays. To every tradition, to every practitioner of every religion, to the spiritual, to the skeptics, to the anal retentive. My best from mine, straight to your butt. Just kidding. I love you.
