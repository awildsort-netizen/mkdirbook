### Joel, Online 

I walk towards the warzone that I used to name "home." Down Sandy, taking a 
right at Cesar Chavez and heading east on Broadway. I touch the elder trees as 
I pass them by, seeking strength, feeling scared of my neighbors with the gun, 
feeling scared of myself. After all, it wasn't my neighbor that tore apart my 
house and marched away from the rubble as if it was a program on a punch 
card. 

No. That was 100% me. 

I remember that day. I ripped apart the art on the walls, I threw a lamp 
through the window, I launched the metal bed frames into the yard. I was my 
own monster. I cannot tell you the internal logic of my mind at the time, but I 
knew myself limitless then. People worry too much about conserving 
something that's in the past or never even existed—but God is an infinite 
source of creation, don't focus too much on the demo. This destructive 
spirit had taken over the controls, and it had its marching orders. Chaos was 
the name of the game, and placing the right pieces on the board for 2026 was 
part of the strategy. 

The day before, I had lost my passport dancing the streets of Portland with the 
Long Ago, and my mood had been painted in broad brushstrokes of grump 
since. Why can't I take care of myself? How could I be so reckless to jump and 
down in pure joy with the dead, in reunion with a lifestream that ran so deep it 
left me drunk and carefree?

Hotels require identity for me to stay; my money is no good without its 
accompanying documentation. The trials we put our immigrant kin through 
sits heavy on my mind. 

I entered my house to look for my other accepted form of identity: my driver's 
license. But it was impossible to find anything in the sea of trash that moved 
through my living room, my bedroom, upstairs, both bathrooms. 

It looked like it was going to be a cold January night out on the city streets, or a 
night at the Broadway house I still owned—every ounce of flesh in my body 
fighting it. 

And then, I turned on my music and started to clean my mess. I transcended—
but ultimately failed—at finding my identity. 

That is, until I called the last hotel I had stayed at and explained my situation. 

They told me to come back. Room 202 was waiting for me. 

It was only THEN that I found my driver's license. That's the moment I came 
online.
