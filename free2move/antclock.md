### Antclock: it’s Semantic

Time was never really ticking.

That sound was for us.

We measure our lives this way already, even if we don’t say it out loud. Before 
the move. After the diagnosis. The moment everything stopped being 
hypothetical. The clock on the wall keeps its steady chant, but human time 
stutters.

The AntClock project started out as what was going to be a class: I generated a 
fast paced world internal, exploring fractals and fractured personalities (all of 
which were my own)—it was befit with a syllabus, a $10,000 infused, digital 
currency and a new way to learn that promise to reward people who just 
didn’t get it as a way of improving the way we taught it.

None of that ended up coming to pass. Outside of my own head.

What has remained though? My respect for ants in my bathroom. There were 
all sorts of reasons I could conjure about why ants were in my bathroom; why 
they seem to dance with me. I can’t still quite explain why they would pause in 
their movements, as if an inquiry of my own motives.

I believe ants really do run on a different clock than we humans do. That there 
never really was a clock that merely counted cycles around an uncaring sun. 
The only sort of clock that’s ever been important is relational, the sort that fills 
our lives with meaning.
