**I DON’T MISS MY DAD ANYMORE**

There are not many people like me. I have been poor. I have been wealthy. My name has been the talk of the town—on the lips of the elite in praise, in dirt-dragged schadenfreude, in hushed reverant tones imagined.

I have not been homeless, but I’ve run scared from my home. I ran to my neighbor to ask for protection, but he told me I was dangerous to his family. I was hungry and cold, so I went to my neighborhood grocery store Whole Foods, where I was turned away and told to leave. I left, walking into traffic, bothering only the traffic. I have needed the kindness and love of my neighborhood, and when I looked for it, I found myself strapped into an ambulance and sent away into a dark room on Christmas.

I had a ten year career as a Software Engineer, ahem, by the close of that chapter of my life: a **Senior Software Engineer,** a leader in good standing with a certain known corporation Google. I attended OSSM, pronounced “awesome,” the Oklahoma School of Science and Mathematics, where I was educated by award winning, respected scholars in Calculus and Engineering, Chemistry, Modern Algebra, and Computer Sciences. This education was offered to me at no cost to me or my family, including

1. a divorced mom who worked as a case manager with the Greer County Health Department
2. a “con artist” dad whose CV I like to imagine would be a photocopy of his ass.

For the first time in about thirty years, I don’t actually have to imagine anymore. I can call my dad up and ask, “Hey Dad. I’m writing a story about where I come from, and I’m just spitballing about how you would describe yourself professionally. Could I get your fax number?”

I cannot impress upon you how healing this is for me. I have been shouting at my mom, my family, cars, social workers, walls, concepts, spacetime: “I MISS MY DAD.” I don’t have to keep screaming because now, I can call my dad whenever I want to.
