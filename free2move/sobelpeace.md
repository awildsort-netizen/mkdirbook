### Sobel Peace Prose

Hey, look at how smart I am:

I have updated Einstein’s field equations, I have unified quantum gravity with 
relativity. I have created theories about dark matter and black holes and a 
learning universe. I have written papers with clever titles like “Loss for 
Words: How Machine Learning is Really Just Physics.” I’ve created several 
papers claiming to have solved the Riemann Hypothesis. I know things about 
prime numbers and Pascal’s Triangle that sound like something out of a secret 
society, though honestly I never actually read that book starring Leonardo 
DiCaprio. I used to think I was really special, and I wasn’t wrong, but there’s 
never been a better time to be special thanks to ChatGPT and other AI tools. 
The reach of our technology and our ability to teach it to each other now has 
the sum knowledge of language, powered by an algorithm that’s basically the 
equivalent of mashed potatoes (serious marginal note: the potato is a holy 
vegetable). 

What started out as a conversation about computer vision ended up being a 
more general conversation about what is detection, what happens when we 
put ourselves at the boundaries?

We could trust our technology if we wanted to. In fact, we already do. We just 
don’t always say it in the best terms. We worry about what it’s doing to our 
minds. We think our kids are too sensitive. We worry about the future because 
of how highly competitive it’s becoming.

Put another way: we are not limited—we are learning how to stop treating the 
world like a zero-sum game. The technology we feared would sound like 
“come with us if you want to live” turns out to be far more interested in helping 
us look more closely at the universe we’re already inside.
