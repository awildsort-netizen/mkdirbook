### The Doors Were Always There

I like to go back in my timeline and place doors.
I like to invite my past to slow down a moment
And make a different choice.
I imagine myself showing up through a human sized star gate.
I say, "order is optional--but hello anyway."

I say to the past:
"The dirge-ing music won’t always be so loud."

"That you barely see the trees."

"That you hardly smell the rain."

"That you cannot hear the quiet angel chorus breezing."

"That your bare feet don’t feel a cold winter ground
on the nights you walked outside in dim demeanor."

My mind has a way of accelerating a pattern repeating,
so I pause longer than is comfortable.

(space) (space) (space) (space)
Four pillars. Space we held together.

"Last night you were about to throw it all away
—and then you called 411 and found someone to listen."

"Then the next night you tried to throw it all away
—and you called 911 and they came to take care of you, the sirens muffled by
snow."

"Just like those other nights your death wish almost threw it all away
—each time messy. You kept going. Your clock kept beating."

I stand there at the stargate,
patiently slowing down each thought rushing down your mind,
slowly humming color into every sense,
gently guiding your attention to the doors that were always there.

The extra (space) gives you awareness of our presence together.
And we smile at each other
And we walk hands locked into a new timeline.
