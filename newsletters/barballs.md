### I Tried to be Barbie

The Internet came to Mangum, Oklahoma around the time I came to puberty—1997 or
so. And if you have the Internet meeting puberty, well, you know exactly what a
fateful meeting that was.

It hit me like a tornado with no forewarning. It was not a fair fight, and I’d
never dug a cellar before. I buried my genitals in the backyard, a deep well of
shame that struck black oil. I fracked it into the deep dark, and at 39 years
old it would gush out of my body like the eighth wonder of hell.

Algebra class with teenagers older than me, but they felt younger and cooler.
My mom comatose in Oklahoma City, my step dad calls her a bitch when he finds
the email correspondence. The high school of science and mathematics and taking
the ACT again and again and again to meet my ambition. My fire and brimstone
third place before we called them third places. My sexuality current drifting
towards naked men in the country, sitting naked under a moonlit sky. My best
friend joining the football team and twisting my arm behind my back as a way to
say, “I have new friends now.” My dad, visions and madness and drugs and
trailer parks.  I had a lot on my plate.

I simply didn’t have time to unbury my genitals. If the devil represents our
physical plane, how light begins to catch into mass as it experiences time,
then I had made a deal with the heavy prince as soon as my hormone tornado
turned the sky green. I gave him a few minutes of my time in a tiny little box,
and put that box away with a shame lock when he had come and gone.

This arrangement worked really well until it didn’t. That’s what I told myself
with my genitalia down in the cellar like a portrait of Dorian Gray that lived
down not up and made of bathroom oil graffiti on a 666 canvas. I thought I
could walk through life without a certain swing in my step, Pandora’s Balls far
away from me because that had nothing to do with me. Back then we thought
everything could be cubed and packed and contained and that animals didn’t have
souls and we could look the other way if we showed up at the pew and acted like
that was all of us sitting there praying and singing and learning about an
angry Poseidon god.

Breathe man, breathe.

I’ve been sober from meth a little over a month now. Oh! Teenage me did an
incredibly difficult feat by the way—he didn’t get anywhere near the stuff. His
mom woke up. He was accepted into Oklahoma School of Science and Mathematics
and left town at 16 years old. He learned both kinds of calculus, did you know
there were two?! He started college with three of his friends from OSSM (yes,
they called it “awesome”) with scholarship and course credits and a summer
internship. He wrote a TCP packet parser and a chat program and reasoned about
bidding agents and one time got free fries when he paid for chicken McNuggets
using mostly nickels and dimes.

But midlife middle named hot mess me, Joel, is just now at that excavation site
with an oily open box of corruption holding Pandora.

Can you tell me what love is? I know it as light, but as mass I’m at a loss.
It’s smelly and confusing with lumpy thighs like cellulite and I find it hard
to love. I keep thinking about the Barbie movie, where a doll chooses to become
real and her first real act is to see her gynecologist.

Can somebody here please tell me what love is?
