### Holding Two Dads

I remember my voice cracking when I screamed at my Dad to never call our house number again, so I must have been eleven or twelve. I remember feeling embarrassed about that. But mostly, I remember how the anger pulsed through my body. My blood felt like plasma, not the leukocyte but the phase state, not boiling but oozing molten chasmic terraform. I've never moved a mountain, but I have pushed two cooled plates apart through the strength granted to me by my guardians.

He never called the house again.

Now, he leaves messages on my voicemail.

I missed my Dad. And at first, the reconnection felt amazing. But it wasn't long before I saw the patterns emerge from my childhood: the impossible to follow constructed reality, the agitation of not being understood, the refusal to explain, the way he knew my first name and my middle name but a stubborn refusal to call me by the one that is mine. And then the hidden truth emerged--he's never known who I was.

His interest in who I am now was a flash of lucidity.

I honestly can't say that his episodes are episodic.

He carries deep, dark curses — haunted corners of American trauma — that now, here in the year of our brown mystic iconista Yeshua, 2026, present themselves as MAGA red rage: veins flaring, gasping for air.

Here's the thing. He's still here. I still love him, I genuinely don't hold any hate in my heart towards him. I'm strong enough, I'm confident enough, I'm unbothered enough to hold measured hope for something different. Because I SAW something different. There is a softer, fascinating man behind this chucklefuck of a walking chaos goblin--and I can hold both of those Dads at the same time. It is my spiritual work, it is the contract I signed up for, it is my ninja way. I don't give up on people, and that includes my Dad.

That day when I screamed at him was the day he threatened to kill my mom if she kept me away from him. She had screamed and dropped the phone, crying uncontrollably. When I asked what happened, she told me. I didn't waste a second to make the call. My error in judgment was actually all these years later, harboring uncertainty about if Mom had been overreacting, if I had been missing my Dad for so long for no reason.

I had made the right call. Even at that age, I could see the family that loved all of me, that saw all of me, that wasn't perfect but never stopped growing, learning, trying to love me every single day no matter what. My mom, my grandma loved me so much that they kept letting this scary angry man into their lives because I was so determined to not give up on him. I pushed plates apart because these women had sacrificed so much on my behalf. They showed me what love is, and I knew in my heart how to move forward even if my brain hadn't caught up.

Today is a different story. I'm a grown man. And I'm strong, observant, confident, curious, and spirited. I walk with the gods, I talk with my ancestors, I wield the math hammer of Thor, and my visions and hallucinations have led me to a unique viewpoint about our universe, consciousness, and our future. I am optimistic because I built it out of my own iron will.

No, I don't excuse his behavior. No, I'm not enabling him. No, I'm not scared or complacent or naive.

I'm just something you've never seen before.
