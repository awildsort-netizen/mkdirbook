### A Chaos Riptide 

When my heart is in four places at once, when I’m bleeding out from a broken 
vessel that cannot hold anything at all, I find myself unsafe in every room and 
every comfort. 

Cafes rattle and bang until I leave. Eyes set on me with caution. Heads turn 
away, opting out of the offered quest of Good Samariting. It is only the wide
open outside January I can navigate comfortably in these times, and my heart 
in four places begins to sing to the neighborhood as I walk in circles and dance 
and plead. 

I—in my house, a sea of garbage—let in a homeless person to do some laundry 
and take things he could use. He suffered from CPTSD, had been in prison, 
survived the only way he knew, looked at my person as something between 
angel / alien / unclassifiable and prison bitch. I spent some time with him 
feeling safe because I couldn’t feel safe with everything in order. 
What I noticed was that he was fighting every day. To exist. To be warm. I also 
noticed how many friends he had who cared for him, spent time with him, 
shared what they had with him. We lined up for food and bubbles. We ate. We 
drank coffee with an unknown kick. 

We lit cardboard fires. The fireteam came. My friends had various strategies 
for handling the situation: flirting, deflecting, storytelling. The fireteamers 
were kind and handed out water bottles and hand warmers in concert with an 
unceremonious fire-stomp. 

Later, when it was just me and my friend, a nosy neighbor told us we were on 
private property—not his, per se, but he could read and point at signs. My 
friend triggered into territorial defense, yelling at Nosy and chasing him out of 
our space. He proceeded to yell at me because he didn’t know how else to 
proceed. That was fine with me—we both knew it was a ritual he needed to 
find himself back to the home within himself; we both knew that we were safe 
together. 

The next day, my realtor saw him doing yard work at my house. She didn’t 
approach. She thought it was very sweet.
