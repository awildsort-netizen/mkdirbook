### I Look to the Mountains

The most dazzling part of finding myself in Palm Springs: I can always look up
at a mountain--make the scale of things. And then, decide.

"Five years without an address. So I swore to myself. I would never let myself
be homeless again. What I do in the privacy of my own room is *none* of their
business." -My southbound friend

"The next day, I went to the Salton Sea... and I just drove around, I walked, I
took pictures, and I became free. It was as if I let something die right there
in that desert." -Linda Perry

I've been lost most of my life. As a kid, I had a strong signal, but through
trying to prove myself to the world, my connection to something deeper lost
power to a deafening cacophony of Other People's Opinions. After wandering in
the spiritual desert for my entire adult life, I fell into addiction. I lost
the thread of extrinsic motivation and stopped volunteering and stopped going
to school. My support system evaporated in front of my eyes, at the time I
needed it the most. I became isolated.

Isolated. But housed in my own home.

Sheltered from many of the consequences of being a crystal meth addict in a
world that only knew redemption through brutal abstinence. I watched in
fascination at the strangers that washed through my home--specifically, the
shame that moved through the room with them.

My southbound friend, his ex, and I walked the cool night streets, down a path
flagged orange in one direction and blue the other. I was falling in love with
the desert. They were moments from their final act together: the unravelling of
love in hopeless places. Sunk low in the valleys, high minds dissipating, an
oasis left to the imagination of the golden.

It would be another week of chaos for me, with hotels in series, with smoke
dancing on little bubble stages. I would continue seeking a new home before I
finally landed somewhere solid--an address I could put on a government form, a
roof over my head of consequence, built on the right bedrock.
