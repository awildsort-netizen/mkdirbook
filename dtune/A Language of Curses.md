### A language of curses

You understand that shame serves a purpose, right? It’s not something
vestigial. It’s not something unfortunate. It is a continuity of the group. You
see, anything that stays around for a while has something of a strategy for
making sure it stays around. As an individual human, something like fear gives
us a continuity regarding tiger chases and spider bites. The feeling of shame
is a group feeling: it helps set the rules that everyone decides to play by,
kind of like the speed limit for people activities.

The individual and the group are intertwined in survival. That is especially
true for humans. When the group has higher chances of coherence, humans in the
group have higher survival ability.

Now that we know how the music is played, we can figure out how to break those
rules in a way that helps us ascend. If you could picture the same as a visual
play, it might look like a roly-poly curling up into a ball. It is a shrinking
feeling. You want to hide. You falter when it comes to explaining yourself. The
words start to move into a slow trickle. Not just from your mouth, but from
your mind. Often times you’d rather just not think about the experience of
shame.

I remember learning about this emotion in dialectical behavioral therapy. It
had these opposite actions that helped clear the emotion. Today I would call
those list transmutative. The suggestion actions were to tell somebody or to do
the thing around somebody. If you’re going for a partial effect, we’re looking
at the world of secrets.

If you think about it, the information I’m telling you now listen in forbidden
magic. Messing with shame is powerful. Why would I tell you the secrets of
secrets? Because shame has one particularly terrible side effect: it makes us
feel bad about thoughts and actions that we’ve inherited, what I would call our
generational curses. Shame is one of the worst feelings to experience when it
comes to generational curses. In fact, shame gives continuity to the curses by
preventing us our progress.

Do you see where I’m going with this? Do you understand why I’ve betrayed the
demon of shame? I need to set the next level. And I love our ancestors for
getting us to where they’ve gotten us to.

But when it comes to inherited trauma and ancestral, shame, it’s time for us to
metabolize that and return it to the universe. Racism has transformed over the
years, but if we want to actually put it to bed, we have to bring it out and
put it under our own spiritual microscopes. Once we have our deep shame out on
the table, it’s no longer a curse.

It’s now a negotiation.
